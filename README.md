My background spans identity provider implementation, large-scale user onboarding, and ServiceNow administration. IAM is the natural evolution of that work — moving from managing access processes to engineering and securing the identity systems that power them.

I'm actively building toward the Microsoft SC-300 (Identity and Access Administrator) certification, with a longer-term path into cloud security and identity governance.

# Why These Labs Matter
Identity and Access Management is no longer a supporting IT function — it is the front line of enterprise security.

In 2026, compromised credentials account for nearly a third of all initial breach vectors (IBM X-Force, 2026). Attackers no longer break into systems; they log in using stolen or misconfigured identities. Meanwhile, organizations are managing a rapidly expanding identity surface: not just employees and contractors, but APIs, bots, service accounts, and autonomous AI agents — non-human identities that now outnumber human users by more than 144 to 1.

The labs in this repository reflect that reality. They are not textbook exercises. They are simulations of the access control problems that IAM engineers solve every day:

- Who should have access to what, and under what conditions?
- How do you enforce least-privilege without breaking workflows?
- How do you detect when access has drifted beyond its original scope?
- How do you govern identities that aren't human at all?

Every lab is documented the way a professional would document real work — with context, configuration decisions, observations, and lessons learned. That's the standard I hold myself to here.
